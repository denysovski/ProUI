# Dashboards and business sites: what good ones share

Where to look: Mobbin web screens and sections (https://mobbin.com, search "dashboard", "analytics",
"settings", "billing", "pricing section", "hero section"; Mobbin needs a paid account and the Mobbin connector
needs a paid plan), Dribbble (https://dribbble.com/tags/dashboard; treat as mood, many shots are unbuildable
concept art), Saved (https://saved.design), and the products themselves: Linear, Stripe Dashboard, Vercel,
Plausible, Notion, Raycast, Attio, Height, Mercury.

## Patterns that recur in well-made product UI

- **Quiet chrome, loud data.** Sidebar and header in a second neutral; colour reserved for data series, status
  and the primary action (`DB-03`, `DB-14`).
- **One page header pattern everywhere:** title, short context line, primary action on the end side, secondary
  actions in a menu.
- **Answer first.** The top of the page is one sentence-sized answer or two to four key numbers with trends,
  not eight equal tiles (`DB-05`, `DB-06`).
- **Tables are the product.** Most real work happens in tables with filters, saved views and bulk actions
  (`DB-09`, `DB-10`).
- **Command menu** (`⌘K`) for navigation and actions; keyboard hints in menus and tooltips (`IX-17`).
- **Empty states with a first step** and sometimes a short video or sample data toggle (`DB-12`).
- **Settings as grouped forms**: left navigation of categories, one column of labelled fields, save per
  section or autosave with confirmation.
- **Restrained type**: one sans family, three or four sizes on screen, weights 400 / 500 / 600.
- **Borders thin and few, radius small (4–8 px)**, shadows only on popovers and dialogs (`PL-09`).

## Patterns that recur in professional business websites

- **A headline that names the outcome** for a specific customer, and the product shown working right under it
  (real UI, not an illustration) (`DC-06`, `DC-09`).
- **Proof close to claims**: a customer logo row only with real customers, a short case with a number, a named
  quote with a role and company (`CP-14`).
- **Pricing that matches the business**, with what is included in plain words and an FAQ under it (`SL-11`).
- **Navigation of four to six items**, one primary button in the header.
- **A footer that works**: contact, legal, status page, social, sitemap-like links.
- **Sections in different compositions**, not a stack of centred blocks (`LY-12`).

## Gradients on professional sites

- One signature gradient tied to the brand colour, used in the hero or a product shot backdrop and repeated in
  the OG image (`GR-02`).
- Gradients usually sit behind real product imagery, giving it light and depth, not behind text paragraphs.
- Dark products tend to use a low-chroma gradient from near-black to a dark brand tint; light products use a
  pale tint fading to the background.
